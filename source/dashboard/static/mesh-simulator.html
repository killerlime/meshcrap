Shared airtime demand

Offered airtime in one shared RF neighborhood, not measured channel utilization or delivery probability. Above 100% means more transmission time is requested than one channel can carry sequentially. Contention can matter well below that.

Why the chirp matters

LoRa encodes symbols with frequency sweeps called chirps. Symbol time is 2⁽ˢᶠ⁾ ÷ bandwidth: higher spreading factors or narrower bandwidth keep each symbol on air longer. More airtime gives the receiver more opportunity to recover weak signals, but also occupies the channel longer.

Negative packet SNR can be normal for LoRa. It is not interchangeable with RSSI or idle noise floor. Received packets are only the successful sample, and a relayed packet's radio readings describe its last hop.

Assumptions and limits

All nodes initially share compatible settings. A change made on one side breaks the modeled link; changing everybody together preserves radio compatibility, not guaranteed delivery. Matching frequency alone is insufficient: modem settings, channel keys and firmware support also matter. Small frequency offsets are conservatively treated as mismatches; receiver tolerance is not modeled.

Link-margin change uses a typical 2.5 dB per spreading-factor step, thermal-noise bandwidth scaling and free-space frequency scaling with identical antenna gains. Coding rate changes airtime but receives no invented sensitivity bonus. The starting margin is your assumption, not a measurement. No terrain, interference, antenna mismatch, receiver noise figure, weather, ducting, routing, collisions or battery-life prediction is included.

Frequency examples are mathematical comparisons, not approved operating plans. Hardware bands, antennas, region rules and firmware settings must support an actual change. A stronger transmitter does not strengthen the other node's return signal. No real node data is loaded or stored.
